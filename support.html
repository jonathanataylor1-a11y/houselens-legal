Looking up homes

  • How it works. Point your iPhone's camera at a house. HouseLens uses your location, compass and camera direction, on your phone, to work out which home you're looking at from the county's free public property records. It then shows the HouseLens Estimate (anchored to the home's Zillow Zestimate when one is available), recent recorded sales or nearby assessor market values where the county publishes them, and a local market trend.
  • "HouseLens doesn't cover this area yet." HouseLens works where a county or state publishes free public property records that the app can read quickly. Coverage grows as more counties are connected. The data sources are listed at sources.html.
  • The wrong house is highlighted. Stand on the sidewalk facing the house, hold the phone upright and wait a moment for the compass to settle. On dense blocks (rowhouses, brownstones) use the ‹ › arrows to step one door, or point at the house number.
  • No account is needed to look up homes, and your location and the homes you look at aren't sent to HouseLens.
  • Estimates are approximate and are not appraisals.

Real estate agents

  • Advertising is under Settings (gear icon) → Advertise with HouseLens. You sign in with your email and a 6-digit code.
  • "HouseLens doesn't offer advertising in your area yet." Your ad can only appear on homes in counties HouseLens covers, so plans can't be bought for other areas. Tap Notify me when it's available to be emailed when your area opens.
  • Manage or cancel a plan in iPhone Settings → your name → Subscriptions. Cancel at least 24 hours before renewal. Apple handles payments and refunds (Apple support).
  • Delete your agent account in the app under Advertise with HouseLens → Delete account. Deleting the account doesn't cancel an Apple subscription; cancel it in Settings too.
  • License verification. New York licenses are checked automatically against state records; other states are checked by a person, usually within a few business days.
