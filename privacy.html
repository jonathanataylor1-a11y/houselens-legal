Summary

  • You don't need an account to scan homes. HouseLens identifies the house you point at using your camera direction, GPS location, compass heading and motion sensors. That calculation happens on your iPhone.
  • Your GPS position, heading, motion data, camera images and scan history are not sent to HouseLens's servers or to our advertising partner. Some outside services do receive limited information so the app can work. For example, Zillow receives the street address of the home you identified. Details are below.
  • Ads are contextual. HouseLens does not ask for App Tracking Transparency permission, so iOS does not give the app or Google your device's advertising identifier (IDFA).
  • Only real estate agents who choose to advertise create accounts. Agent data is stored with our hosting provider, Supabase. Agents can delete their account in the app.
  • We do not sell personal information.

1. Who this policy covers

  • Consumers who use HouseLens to identify and look up homes. Consumers never sign in.
  • Real estate agents who sign in to publish a sponsored profile and buy an advertising subscription ("agents").

2. Information that stays on your device

HouseLens uses the following on your iPhone to identify a home. It is not uploaded to HouseLens:

  • Camera. The live camera view is used to aim at a house. Camera frames are not uploaded. When you scan a home, the app may save one small photo (at most 480 pixels) from the camera as the thumbnail for that scan, stored only on your device.
  • Location, heading and motion. Your GPS location, compass heading and device motion are used on the device to work out which building you are pointing at. iOS asks your permission before the app can use your location or camera.
  • Recent Scans. The app keeps your most recent scans (up to 15 homes) on your device so you can reopen them. Each one holds the property record, the estimate shown, comparable homes, any Zillow value and the thumbnail. You can delete one scan by swiping it, or delete all of them with Clear in Recent Scans. Deleting the app deletes them too.
  • Caches. To avoid repeating lookups, the app keeps on your device Zestimates it has already read (up to 500 homes, refreshed after 72 hours) and computed estimates (usable for up to 7 days). These are deleted when you delete the app.
  • Other on-device records. The app keeps a local log of sponsored-card events (for example "card shown"). It also keeps a list of homes already counted today for the activity counter (Section 3.6), stored as salted one-way hashes rather than property IDs. Any lead request you submit is also stored on the device (Section 4).

If no camera frame is available (for example, the camera is off), the thumbnail may be a satellite map image of the property's location from Apple Maps (Section 3.3).

3. Information that leaves your device

3.1 Public property-record services

[DRAFT CHANGE, October 14, 2026: nationwide coverage.] First, the app works out which county you are in on your device, from U.S. Census Bureau county boundaries built into the app. No request is made for this.

To find the buildings and addresses around you, the app then downloads public property data directly from the public GIS service of that county or state (for example Dutchess County's service on Esri's ArcGIS Online, the New York State parcel service, or the statewide parcel services of states such as Connecticut, Florida or Maryland). The services HouseLens uses are listed at houselens-legal/sources.html. Each request contains the boundaries of a small map area (cells about 250 meters across) around your current position. It does not contain your exact GPS reading or any HouseLens identifier, but it does show the general area you are in. Where a layer has no ZIP code, the app looks it up on your device from Census ZIP areas built into the app.

In some counties, recorded home sales are published only as bulk files. For those counties the app downloads a sales file for the whole county from HouseLens's file storage (Supabase Storage). The request names the county, never your position, and is the same for everyone in that county.

To read assessment facts for the identified home and nearby comparable homes, the app sends those properties' coordinates to the New York State Tax Assessment Lookup service (gisservices.its.ny.gov).

Like any internet request, these services also receive your device's IP address and standard technical information. Their own policies govern what they log.

3.2 Zillow

After a home is identified, the app loads that home's public Zillow web page in a hidden in-app browser and reads the Zillow "Zestimate" shown there. Zillow receives:

  • the street address, city, state and ZIP code of the identified home, taken from the public property record (never from your GPS), as part of the page address;
  • your IP address and the standard information any web browser sends.

The Zillow page's own scripts run in that hidden browser and may set cookies or contact other services, as they would in Safari. HouseLens limits this. The page cannot read your location (location requests are refused). Images, video and fonts are not downloaded. Cookies last only until the app is closed. HouseLens is not affiliated with Zillow, and Zillow's privacy policy applies to what Zillow collects. To turn this off, [OWNER TO CONFIRM: no in-app switch currently exists for consumers].

3.3 Apple

  • Apple Maps may receive a property's coordinates if the app creates a satellite map thumbnail for a scan.
  • Apple DeviceCheck. See Section 3.6.
  • App Store. Agent purchases are processed by Apple (Section 5).

3.4 Sponsored agent lookup (HouseLens servers)

When a home is identified, the app sends the five-digit ZIP code of that home to HouseLens's server (hosted by Supabase) to find agents who are advertising in that ZIP. The server returns each agent's card and, for agents who advertise within a radius, the agent's map pin and radius. The app then checks on your iPhone whether the identified home is inside each agent's radius and picks the card there. Agent headshots are then downloaded from our storage. No location, heading, address, property ID or scan history is sent.

3.5 Sponsored card statistics (HouseLens servers)

When a paid sponsored card is shown, opened, or you tap Call, Text or Email, the app sends a counter event. It contains a random event ID, the campaign ID, the event type, the home's ZIP code, where in the app it happened, and the time. It does not include a device identifier, location, address or anything about you. Agents see these as totals.

3.6 HouseLens activity counter (HouseLens servers)

In the App Store version, when the camera identifies a real home, the app reports one "property identification" per home per day. Reopening a saved scan does not count. The report contains only:

  • a random one-time ID;
  • the home's five-digit ZIP code;
  • when available, an Apple DeviceCheck token. Our server sends it to Apple to confirm the request came from a genuine Apple device. HouseLens cannot use this token to identify you or your device.

To limit abuse, our server combines your IP address with a secret value and the date to make a one-way hash. The raw IP address is not stored by our code. The hashes are deleted after about one day, and the one-time IDs after about three days. What we keep is a count of identifications per ZIP code per day. Agents see these counts for their ZIP codes, and counts under 10 are hidden.

3.7 Advertising (Google)

HouseLens shows a banner ad through the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob) and uses Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) for consent:

  • HouseLens marks ad requests as non-personalized. It does not attach your location, keywords about the home, or any identifiers to the request.
  • HouseLens does not request App Tracking Transparency permission. iOS therefore does not give the app or Google your advertising identifier (IDFA).
  • Google's SDK still collects some information on its own, such as your IP address, device and app information, and ad interactions. Google uses it to deliver ads, limit how often you see them, measure them and prevent fraud. Google's own policies govern this: see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.
  • Where Google determines consent is required (for example in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland), Google's consent form is shown before ads are requested. [OWNER TO CONFIRM: whether US state privacy messages are enabled in the AdMob console, and whether the app will offer a "Privacy options" button to change consent later.]

HouseLens never adds your GPS position, heading, motion data, the address you scanned, your scan history or your contact details to ad requests.

3.8 What HouseLens's servers never receive from consumers

HouseLens's servers and our advertising partner do not receive your GPS coordinates, compass heading, motion data, camera images, the exact address you scanned, your scan history, or your contact details. The one exception is a lead request you choose to send (Section 4). As explained above, Zillow does receive the identified home's address, and public GIS services receive map areas and property coordinates.

4. Contacting a sponsored agent

  • Call, Text, Email. These buttons open your phone's own Phone, Messages or Mail app with the agent's published number or email. HouseLens does not write a message for you and does not see or store what you send. Whatever you send goes directly to the agent. HouseLens only counts that the button was tapped (Section 3.5).
  • Request forms ("Request a Tour", "Ask a Question"). Where offered, you can send the agent your first name, phone number and/or email, an optional message, whether you want a tour or similar homes, and how you prefer to be contacted. The request includes the address and ZIP of the home you were viewing, the agent and campaign, and the time you agreed to the consent text shown. It is saved on your device first. It is then sent to HouseLens's lead-delivery service, which forwards it to the agent shown. If it can't be delivered, it stays on your device, the app tells you it hasn't been sent, and it retries a limited number of times. 5. Real estate agent accounts

If you sign in to advertise, we collect:

  • Sign-in email. You sign in with a one-time code sent to your email. Codes are sent by Supabase Auth through our email provider, Brevo.
  • License number at sign-up and the license lookup. When you start, you enter your email, license state and license number. After you enter the code, the app sends the license state and number to HouseLens's server (hosted by Supabase). For New York, the server looks the number up in the New York Department of State's public open-data records of active licenses (data.ny.gov) and returns the public record to your app: the licensee name, license type, brokerage, office address and expiry date. The app uses it to fill in your profile, and you confirm whether it's you. The lookup itself doesn't save the record to your profile; only what you then publish is saved. To limit misuse, the server keeps a hashed per-account counter of lookups for rate limiting. For other states, no lookup is made and you enter your details yourself.
  • Looking up your own public agent profile. After you tap This is me on a New York license record, the app looks for your own public agent profile, on your device, so it can suggest your photo, phone and email. It uses the name, brokerage and state from that record. If your brokerage is eXp Realty, it opens eXp's public agent search (exprealty.com) with your name and state. For any brokerage, it opens Zillow's public agent directory (zillow.com) with your name and state, and, if exactly one agent there matches, that agent's public Zillow profile page. These pages load in a hidden in-app browser with the same limits as in Section 3.2: they can't read your location, cookies last only for that lookup, and video, fonts and most images aren't downloaded. eXp and Zillow receive your name and license state as search terms, plus the network information any website receives (such as your IP address). Their own scripts may run as they would in Safari, including eXp's invisible reCAPTCHA check. HouseLens suggests something only if exactly one listed agent matches the name on your license and also matches your brokerage (or, on Zillow, your license office city). It then downloads that agent's public profile photo to your device, and reads the phone and email that agent's Zillow profile publishes; your brokerage's main phone line is never used. The search gives up silently after about 10 seconds, or if a site shows a challenge or blocks the request; it isn't retried. Nothing found is sent to HouseLens's servers or used unless you choose Use this photo or Use these. A photo you use then goes through the headshot steps below, and you can still edit the phone and email. HouseLens doesn't keep the search results.
  • Public profile: display name, brokerage, optional bio, optional website, optional public email, a required public business phone that you agree to show, an optional headshot, and where your ad shows: a map pin (stored to about 100 meters) and a radius, or, for earlier accounts, the ZIP codes you serve. Your pin and radius are sent to consumers' apps so their app can decide on the device whether a home is inside your area.
  • Private details: legal name, business email, license state, license type and license number. These are never shown to consumers. To publish a profile, HouseLens verifies the license against the official records of the state that issued it. For New York licenses, HouseLens sends the license number to the New York Department of State's public open-data service (data.ny.gov), compares the returned license record with the legal and display names, and repeats the lookup daily while the profile is published. For other states and the District of Columbia, a HouseLens reviewer looks up the license number and name on that state's official public license lookup. We keep the result (verified or not, the date checked, the license type and expiry date, and for manual reviews who reviewed it, when, and any reason a license couldn't be verified, which we show to the agent). Only the words "License verified" appear on the card; the license state and number are never shown.
  • Confirmations: when you confirmed your details are accurate, accepted the Terms of Use (and which version), acknowledged this policy, and agreed to show your phone publicly.
  • Headshot. You can use the photo suggested from your own public agent profile (above), take a photo with the camera, or choose one from your photo library; the app only receives the photo you pick. On your device, the photo is cropped square, resized to at most 512 pixels, and stripped of location and camera metadata before upload. Until you publish, it is stored privately. Once published, it is stored in a public storage location so consumers' apps can show it.
  • Subscription records from Apple: a random account token that links your purchase to your profile, Apple's transaction ID, product, environment (production or test), status, renewal and expiry dates, and Apple's signed purchase data. Apple processes your payment; HouseLens never receives your card or billing details. Apple's notifications about renewals, refunds and cancellations are recorded.
  • Campaign data and statistics: your ad area (pin and radius, or target ZIPs), your plan (Standard, Plus or Premier) and the rotation weight it sets, whether your card is on or paused, and the aggregate counts described in Sections 3.5 and 3.6.
  • Sign-in session. Your sign-in session is stored in the iOS Keychain on your device.
  • Advertising waitlist (optional). [DRAFT CHANGE, October 14, 2026.] If HouseLens doesn't offer advertising in your area yet, you can tap Notify me when it's available. We then store your sign-in email address and the ZIP code and/or county you asked about, linked to your account so it is deleted when you delete your account. We use it only to email you when HouseLens covers that area. Nothing about consumers is stored with it.

While your profile is published and your subscription is active, your display name, brokerage, headshot, public phone, public email, bio and website are shown to consumers looking at homes in your ad area.

The "estimated monthly home-shopping audience" shown to agents comes from public U.S. Census Bureau data (American Community Survey). It contains no personal information about anyone. The estimated monthly views shown for each plan are calculated from the aggregate per-ZIP property-identification counts in Section 3.6, the share of each ZIP's land inside your area, and the other sponsored agents whose areas overlap yours; they contain no information about any consumer. The number of homes in your area comes from public U.S. Census Bureau data.

9. Your choices and rights

  • Permissions: you can turn off camera or location access in iOS Settings. HouseLens can't identify homes without them.
  • Recent Scans: delete one or all in the app.
  • Delete your agent account: in the app, open Settings (gear icon) → Advertise with HouseLens → Delete agent account. This can't be undone. Deleting your account does not cancel your App Store subscription. Cancel it in Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions on your iPhone.
  • Agents can edit their profile or take their card offline at any time in the app.
  • Requests: to ask what we hold about you, or to correct or delete it, email houselens.app@gmail.com. We may need to verify your request. Consumers don't have accounts, so we usually hold nothing that identifies them.

10. Security

Connections to our servers use HTTPS. Agent data is protected by per-account database access rules. Agent sign-in sessions are kept in the iOS Keychain, and on-device files use iOS data protection. No system is perfectly secure, and we can't guarantee absolute security.
